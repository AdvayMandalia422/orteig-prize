      Debrief · May 20–21, 1927

      How Lindbergh built the Spirit of St. Louis

      • One long wing. Less drag than a biplane, so each gallon went farther.
      • Wright Whirlwind J-5C. 223 horsepower, air-cooled, and reliable for 33½ hours.
      • 450 gallons of fuel. The main tank sat in front of the cockpit, so he looked ahead with a periscope.
      • Alone. No co-pilot, radio, or parachute, all to save weight. He stayed awake about 33 hours in the air, after a night without sleep.
      • Result: took off at 7:52 a.m. from Roosevelt Field and landed at Le Bourget 33 h 30 m later.

      What your class built

        Talk about it

        • Why did Lindbergh fly alone, when other teams brought two to four people?
        • The Liberty engine had almost twice the power of the Whirlwind. Why couldn't it reach Paris?
        • Six men died going after the Orteig Prize before Lindbergh won it. Was the prize worth it?
        • Lindbergh won because he got there first, not fastest. How did the prize's rules shape the race?
